 Lecture 11A: Case Studies in Dexterous Manipulation - Supervised Learning
                                                      Abstract
           This lecture focuses on the latest developments in learning manipulation. The key themes include
       supervised learning and reinforcement learning. The dominant paradigm currently is supervised learning,
       as seen in various efforts by companies like Google and Physical Intelligence, and various research labs.
       However, Prof. Malik believes that multiple approaches are necessary; supervised learning is not the
       final answer to all robotic manipulation challenges. The lecture emphasizes interactive discussion, with
       references to state-of-the-art papers from 2024.


19.1      Introduction: Learning Manipulation Approaches
In the field of robotic manipulation today, there are two primary approaches for training robots to perform
tasks:

   1. Supervised Learning

   2. Reinforcement Learning (RL)

The current leading approach in learning manipulation is supervised learning. Typically, the methodology
involves:

   1. Collecting teleoperated demonstration data.

   2. Training policies using Action Conditioned Transformers (ACT) or diffusion models.

19.2      Teleoperation and Policies Training
One of the first steps in the supervised learning pipeline involves teleoperation, where humans control robots
to perform tasks. The data collected is then used to train policies.
Diffusion policies, which are probabilistic models that can generate actions from observations, have also
shown promising results. Recently, diffusion policies have gained popularity and are becoming a standard.
This is because the goal is a generative model and a multimodal policy— multimodal not in the sense of
multi-sensory, but modes as in statistics. For example, if you are moving towards a goal and there’s an
obstacle in the middle, you can go on the left or right, but if you average these, you might fail. Diffusion
policies help in modeling such multimodal action distributions.

19.3      Hardware Solutions
Several hardware solutions have been developed to facilitate data collection for advanced robotic manipulation.

19.5    Data Collection and Policy Training
In line with supervised learning, humans perform tasks to collect trajectories, which are then used to train
policies. This is analogous to computer vision where you label objects in an image. Given a certain observation,
you are trying to predict an action (e.g., joint angles or changes in joint angles). Trajectories collected are
usually on the order of thousands of demonstrations.

19.6    Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT)
The Action-Conditioned Transformer (ACT) was used in ALOHA (Zhao et al. (2023)) to learn from the
demonstrations collected. It leverages transformer architectures to map observations to actions. See Figure
81 for the architecture.

19.7    Diffusion Policies
Diffusion policies are another approach where a diffusion process is used to model the distribution of actions
conditioned on observations. Recently, diffusion policies have gained popularity because they can model
complex, multimodal action distributions and generate smooth and feasible action sequences.

19.9    Discussion
Critical questions arise from these developments:

19.10     Generalization
Generalization: Can the system generalize across variations in size, shape, appearance, view- point, objects,
lighting, occlusion, and camera perspectives? Will the trained policies generalize to entirely new scenes?
Methods should generalize across different objects of different sizes, shapes, and appearances.

19.11     Robustness
Robustness: How robust is the control system? Can it recover from failures? What are the scaling laws
concerning diverse data? Handling when things go wrong or errors occur is essentially a control problem.
Feedback control handles when things go slightly wrong. Visual imitation learning could address robustness
issues. Datasets like Ego4D, which collect very diverse data, emphasize the importance of diversity for scaling
and improving performance.

19.12     Scaling Laws
A main question here is about scaling behavior:

  1. What are the scaling laws?


                                                     112
   2. Are scaling laws applicable to robotics tasks as they are in vision and language?
   3. Is scaling the answer to everything?
The hidden presumption of scaling laws is that getting more data is getting more diverse data.
There are three main approaches to getting data:
   • Teleoperation (Professor Malik thinks this may be the weakest in terms of diversity).
   • Reinforcement learning through simulation
   • Visual imitation e.g. videos from Youtube.

19.13     More Takeaways
   1. Parallel jaw grippers work for now but we should go towards multifingered hands with tactile sensing.
   2. The future of learning manipulation needs to include scaling diverse datasets through innovative
      collection methods.

19.15     Conclusion
Despite current limitations, the supervised learning paradigm has achieved significant milestones in task
diversity and performance. Advancements in supervised learning approaches, particularly with methods like
ACT and diffusion policies, have shown that robots can perform a wide range of challenging tasks. However,
generalization and robustness remain open questions. Addressing these challenges requires:
   • Scaling diverse datasets through innovative collection methods.
   • Advancing multi-modal policies with multi-fingered hands and tactile sensing.
   • Developing models that can adapt to new and unforeseen situations.
