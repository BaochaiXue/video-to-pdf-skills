3.5    Euclidean transformations
In robotics, we often work on multi-rigid body systems. The key feature of these systems is choosing two
parts in a rigid body, their distance will not change.

Definition 1. Euclidean transformations (isometrics1 ) are transformations that preserve distances
between pairs of points:
                                 ∥ψ(a) − ψ(b)∥ = ∥a − b∥.                                      (1)

Example 1. Translations ψ(a) = a + b are isometrics, since:

                                   ∥ψ(a) − ψ(b)∥ = ∥a + t − (b + t)∥ = ∥a − b∥.                          (2)

3.6    Parameterizing Rotations
Recall that rotation matrices have the property that each column vector has length 1 and the dot product of
any 2 different columns is 0. These 6 constraints leave only 3 degrees of freedom. Here are some alternative
notations used to represent orthogonal matrices in 3-D:

   • Euler angles which specify rotations about 3 axes

   • Axis plus the amount of rotation

   • Quaternions which generalize complex numbers from 2-D to 3-D. (Note, a complex number can represent
     a rotation in 2-D)

3.8    Rigid Body Motion Representation
Theorem 1. Any rigid body motion can be expressed as an orthogonal transformation followed by a translation:

                                               ψ(a) = Aa + t.                                            (7)

Theorem 2. The composition of two isometries is an isometric.
Proof. Given any two isometries ψ1 (a) = A1 a + t1 , ψ2 (a) = A2 a + t2 , we have

                                    ψ1 ◦ ψ2 (a) = A1 (A2 a + t2 ) + t1
                                               = A1 A2 a + A1 t2 + t1
                                               = (A1 A2 )a + (A1 t2 + t1 )
                                               = A3 a + t3                                             (8a)

where A3 = A1 A2 and t3 = A1 t2 + t1 .
