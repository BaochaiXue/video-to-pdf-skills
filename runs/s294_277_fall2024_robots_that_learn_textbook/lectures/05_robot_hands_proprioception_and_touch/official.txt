Joint and Actuation design

Motors as Joints
 • Motors are chained together to form the
   hand itself.
   • Servos have motors, gearbox, angle sensor,
     controllers are packaged together.
 • The output of the servo shafts are used
   directly as the joints and the motors are
   linked together simply.
 • LEAP Hand, Allegro Hand, D’Manus Hand

Strength Issues
• Very easy and inexpensive to produce
• Strong Motors are bulky.
• Small Motors are too weak.
• Kinematic structure is dictated by the
  motors
• This is not how the human hand
  works……a lot of the strength comes
  through our wrists and tendons.

Linkage Joint

  • 4 Bar Linkage is very common
  • RRRR is commonly used with 4
    revolute joints (no prismatic/P)
  • One joint is actuated by a motor
    attached to the joint
  • Can define the motion at the end-
    effector closed-form.
  • Inspire Hand, Psyonic Hand

Linkage Design Characteristics
• Very reliable and accurate actuation
• Linkages do not have much compliance
• However:
  • Many small components can be brittle
  • Low-DOF

Tendon- Driven Hands
• Motors are attached to a tendon and pulley system
• The tendon is often fishing line or a rope
• These tendons pull on various parts of the fingers
• Some similarities to how human tendons work to move muscle
  strength from the forearm to the hand

Revolute Joint
• Two rigid bodies connected and rotate
  against each other
• Only allows for one degree of freedom,
  rotation
• Can be brittle to out-of-axis forces

Rolling Joints
• Rolling joints allow for partial
  motion in another axis other
  than the actuation axis
• When joint hits the table, the
                                     Faive Hand ETH Zurich
  finger can bend and translate to
  avoid breakage
• Typically made with sliding
  contact surfaces and ligaments

                                     Fllex Hand

Flexure Joints
• “Living Hinge” joint characterized
  by a thin piece of plastic/rubber
  material between two rigid bodies
• Allows for rotation and some
  translation on multiple non-
  actuated axis
• Often can be casted or 3D printed

Tendons are very popular!
 • RoboNaut Hand
 • Shadow Hand
 • DLR Hand
 • Faive Hand
 • DASH Hand, LEAP Hand v2
 • Tesla
 • 1X
 • Sanctuary

Hydraulic Robot Hands
• Hydraulic fluid is pumped into a reservoir
  at high pressure
• Valves are used to control the pressure
• Hand actuates from the pressure of the
  fluids actuating different joints
• High strength so the hand is compact

Proprioception
 • Aristotle in “De Anima” listed 5 senses: sight, hearing, touch,
   taste and smell.
 • Bell (1826) proposed a sixth sense – the “muscular sense”,
   which was renamed as “proprioception” by Sherrington.
 • Proprioception refers to the awareness of the position or
   movement of the body or parts of the body relative to each other
    • Musculoskeletal system : sensors in muscles, tendons and joints
    • Vestibular system in the inner ear: semicircular canals & otoliths

The term “kinesthesia” can be used if we ignore the vestibular
system

In robots..

• Joints are driven by electric motors
  where a shaft rotates and applies a
  torque in response to a control input
• A rotary encoder senses the angle
• From these we can compute the
  various joint angles and velocities

The sense of touch
• When the skin is contacted by an external stimulus, its surface
  is indented or stretched as the skin is flexible.
• The mechanical deformation is detected by mechanoreceptors
  that signal location of contact, force exerted, and motion speed.

The sense of touch
• Our subjective perception is that of light touch or pressure, and
  for a moving stimulus that of stroking or vibration.
• Other receptors underneath the skin sense warmth, cold, pain,
  itching.
