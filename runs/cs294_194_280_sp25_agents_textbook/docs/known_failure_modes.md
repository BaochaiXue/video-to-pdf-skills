# Known Failure Modes

These are the failure patterns future lecture and book agents should actively avoid.

1. Dense reasoning slides reduced to one takeaway sentence.
2. Formula pages copied into prose without symbol-by-symbol explanation.
3. Tool-use or coding-agent lectures that mention a workflow but never explain where environment feedback enters.
4. Security chapters that describe capabilities without discussing exploit paths, privilege boundaries, or failure modes.
5. Formal-math chapters that blur informal reasoning, formal specification, proof search, verification, and autoformalization into one concept.
6. Multimodal chapters that mention web, GUI, and OS benchmarks without clarifying environment differences.
7. Readings integrated as a bibliography dump rather than as support for the lecture narrative.
8. Low-confidence transcript spans silently completed with made-up details.
9. Figures inserted for aesthetics rather than teaching value.
10. Cross-lecture terminology drift, especially around reasoning, planning, search, RLHF, DPO, and theorem proving.
11. Book merge that preserves repetitive lecture boilerplate instead of adding cross-chapter transitions.
12. Local helper scripts silently skipping `lecXX_*` workspaces because they assume numeric directory prefixes.
